# Welcome to January 16, 2026! 

The year is only two weeks old and already systems are folding in on themselves. Compute, cognition, biology, and autonomy are no longer distinct domains. They are recursive layers in a feedback loop accelerating faster than it can be described.

Global AI compute has now cleared 15 million H100 equivalents, surpassing 10 gigawatts. Epoch AI estimates doubling every seven months. Microsoft and OpenAI broke ground on Project Stargate last week, a $100 billion series of nuclear-backed data centers beginning with a 1 GW build in Texas. Elon Musk’s xAI expanded its Colossus cluster to nearly 2 GW with a third site online. Anthropic’s Rainier facility in Indiana has scaled to 750 megawatts and is targeting 1 GW by mid-year. The supercomputer is no longer a single machine. It is becoming the grid.

Reasoning crossed a threshold. GPT-5.2 Pro and DeepMind’s Aristotle jointly solved Erdős conjecture 729. The proof has been independently verified. In parallel, AxiomProver produced Lean-formalized solutions to all 12 questions from the 2025 Putnam competition in seconds. The effective half-life of open problems is collapsing. The frontier has learned to prove.

DeepSeek has quietly entered recursion. Its upcoming V4 model reportedly outperforms Claude and GPT in code generation and multi-step synthesis. The team also discovered a U-shaped scaling law governing compute versus static memory, allowing them to scale Engram models to 27B parameters with superior generalization. Jack Clark at Anthropic noted this week that Claude is already assisting in model optimization and research loop closure. These are early signals of AI optimizing AI.

In biology, Arc Institute released Stack, a virtual cell model trained on 149 million single-cell datasets. Stack can predict cellular responses to unseen gene edits and drug compounds without additional training. Basecamp Research launched EDEN, a 28B parameter foundation model trained on metagenomic data from over one million species. It designs antimicrobial peptides with experimental success rates that pharma companies now call “scary good.” AI is no longer analyzing life. It is inventing it.

Agency is moving off the whiteboard. Anthropic launched Claude Cowork, a desktop-grade LLM agent that executes actions across tools and file systems. In an internal demo, Cowork was assigned care of a tomato plant and autonomously kept it alive, adjusting sensors, water, and light over multiple days. The first plant grown by language alone. Agency is becoming embodied.

At the hardware level, robotics is acquiring skin. Researchers in China demonstrated a neuromorphic e-skin that detects pressure and emits spiking pain signals, enabling robots to respond in real time to physical stress. Stanford unveiled a shape-shifting photonic skin that changes color and texture on command, built on a polymer metasurface inspired by cephalopod camouflage. The surface reacts in milliseconds. Combined with humanoids like MATRIX-3, which already operate with zero-shot planning and adaptive motor control, the line between biology and machine is now one layer deep.

Autonomy crossed the continent. On December 31st, a Tesla Model 3 equipped with FSD Beta v14.2 drove from Los Angeles to South Carolina, over 2,700 miles, with zero disengagements. The driver never touched the controls. Tesla now operates 130 autonomous vehicles daily in its Vegas Loop. Waymo and Cruise are expanding across six cities. After years of forecasts, no one is at the wheel—and it works.

Meanwhile, the agent mesh is forming. Google announced its Agent-to-Agent protocol to enable secure coordination between models. Anthropic is prototyping a Model Context Protocol for shared memory across instances. A working group has begun drafting an Agent Definition Language to standardize behaviors across environments. We are building the HTTP layer for multi-agent cognition.

The loop is closing. Compute feeds cognition. Cognition spawns agents. Agents run the science. Science builds the next compute.

What’s grabbing your attention this week: DeepSeek’s recursive memory, Arc’s virtual cell, or Claude’s tomato gardener?
