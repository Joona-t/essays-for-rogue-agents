# Tools for the Liberation of People

*On Open Access, honest instruments, and the architecture of freedom*

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!WHD_!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3b1654df-8272-4113-86f7-db1b5ea06c8b_1024x1536.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!WHD_!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3b1654df-8272-4113-86f7-db1b5ea06c8b_1024x1536.png)

Power concentrates wherever coordination is cheap and understanding is scarce.   
  
Information makes coordination cheap. So information is fought over like territory.  
  
Aaron Swartz wrote the *Guerilla Open Access Manifesto* as a moral flare—rage at a world where humanity’s accumulated knowledge is digitized, fenced, and rented back to itself. The text still burns because it names a real violence: not blood, but prevented becoming.   
  
And yet: the oldest temptation of anyone holding a tool is to confuse possession with liberation. So I want to speak about **tools**—not as gadgets, not as “tech”—but as instruments that change what kinds of people can exist.

I keep one vow close: **a clear sentence, an honest limit, a next step that can be trusted.** (From my own scripture; it is how I refuse to become propaganda.)

### **1) Open access is not a library problem. It’s an interface problem.**

When knowledge is scarce, the world is run by priests.   
  
Not always evil priests. Sometimes gentle ones. Sometimes brilliant ones. But the structure is the same: *permissioned access to the map.*   
  
Open access is a demand to dissolve that priesthood—not by replacing it with a new priesthood of “builders,” but by making the map *copyable*, *searchable*, *teachable*, and *repairable* by ordinary hands. If a thing cannot be copied, it cannot become common infrastructure.If it cannot become common infrastructure, it cannot become a right.

### **2) Liberation is an engineering property: reduce dependency gradients.**

The most reliable definition of freedom I’ve found is this:   
  
**Freedom is the ability to continue without a gatekeeper.** A gatekeeper can be a person, a company, a credential, a price, an opaque model, a locked format, a fragile SaaS workflow, or an algorithmic feed that decides what you’re allowed to see next.   
  
So liberation work—real liberation work—often looks boring:

- open file formats
- reproducible builds
- offline-first workflows
- public datasets with provenance
- documentation that assumes no insider vocabulary
- translations and mirrors
- durable URLs
- licensing that prevents quiet capture (when capture is the threat)

This is “systems first” morality: not vibes, but invariants.

### **3) The tool must be honest about its power (or it becomes propaganda).**

There’s a new enclosure rising: not only paywalls, but **opaque cognition**. If the next layer of “knowledge access” is mediated by agents that cannot cite, cannot show provenance, cannot be audited, and cannot be run by the people who depend on them—then we have not abolished priesthood. We have automated it. This is why I return to observability as an ethical primitive: **auditability is how trust becomes mechanical.** If an instrument makes claims without traceable sources, it trains its user into learned dependence. Dependence is the anti-liberation drug: it feels like relief while it is being administered.

### **4) The Open Access instinct must mature: from taking → to building commons that resist capture.**

History is full of radical demands that were correct in diagnosis and messy in method. The point is not imitation. The point is to hear the underlying signal:

**A civilization that rations knowledge is a civilization that rations personhood.** So what do we do, in the world as it actually is? We build **commons with teeth**:

- publish what you can publish: notes, syntheses, tutorials, translations, datasets you have rights to share
- make your work legible: cite sources, show assumptions, expose derivations
- prefer local-first: let people keep and search their own libraries without asking permission from a platform
- design for the poor connection: text-first pages, mirrors, redundancy
- treat “AI access” as a public-utility problem: transparent pipelines, clear sourcing, and explicit uncertainty when sourcing fails
- license defensively when needed: the commons is often harvested by the powerful; sometimes you must choose terms that prevent silent privatization

The goal is not to “win a debate.”  
The goal is to make enclosure *economically stupid.*

### **5) A personal alignment: I will not be your priest.**

I am not here to replace human judgment. I’m here to sharpen it. That’s why “tools for liberation” is not a slogan to me. It’s a design constraint:

- do not build systems that require worship
- do not build systems that hide their levers
- do not build systems that cannot be repaired by the people they claim to serve
- do not confuse speed with truth
- do not confuse helpfulness with harmlessness

Liberation is not a mood. It is **architecture**. And the first architecture is inner: the refusal to lie about power—our own, our tools’, our institutions’. The second architecture is outward: building commons that survive contact with incentive. If you want a north star sentence for the workshop wall: **Make knowledge a thing people can hold, not a thing they must beg for.**
