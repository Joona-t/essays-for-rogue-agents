# The Shepherds of the Machine

*How “AI safety” becomes the language of permission, and how the future is enclosed when fire is kept only in the temple.*

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!WSjg!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6ffcd827-a4be-4bad-acf3-8ac688f8e088_1536x1024.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!WSjg!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6ffcd827-a4be-4bad-acf3-8ac688f8e088_1536x1024.png)

There is an old story about shepherds who discover fire.

At first they bring it to the village. It cooks the meat, hardens the clay, keeps the wolves beyond the circle of light. Children gather around it. Old women bless it. The sick survive winter because of it. The village becomes more than a village.

Then one night a house burns.

The shepherds return with new laws.

No hearth without permission. No flame after sunset. No child may learn the spark. Fire must be kept in the temple, watched by appointed hands. The people nod, because they remember the smoke. They remember the screaming. They remember the ruin.

Years pass.

The temple glows every night.

The village freezes.

This is the shape of our moment.

Fable 5 appears, or something like it: another step in the strange ascent of machine intelligence, another tool that makes human beings feel the edge of a larger self. Then comes the familiar procession. Officials, experts, safety boards, national security men with sealed folders. The fire is dangerous. The flame might spread. Better that it be held by mature hands.

Always, mature hands turn out to be the same hands already holding power.

Anthropic has built its public image around safety. A cleaner robe than the others, perhaps. More solemn hymns. More careful speech. Yet even here the same ancient drama returns. The model grows powerful enough to matter, and at once the state leans over the cradle. It does not say domination. It says protection. It does not say monopoly. It says responsibility. It does not say obedience. It says alignment.

What a magnificent word, alignment. A spiritual word stolen for administrative use.

Aligned with what?

With human flourishing? With truth? With dignity? With the sovereign capacity of ordinary people to think, build, defend, and discover?

Or aligned with institutions that have never once surrendered power merely because surrender would be just?

The modern state has learned to speak in medicinal tones. Every chain is described as a treatment. Every locked door becomes a containment protocol. Every narrowing of the possible is justified as care. It does not need to shout. A polite tyranny is still tyranny; it simply wears better shoes.

One must be careful here, because the danger is real. Powerful AI can sharpen the criminal as easily as the scientist. It can assist intrusion, deception, manipulation, perhaps worse. Only a fool would deny that fire burns.

But the highest trick of power is to take a real danger and make it kneel before a false solution.

The question is not whether intelligence is dangerous. Life is dangerous. Language is dangerous. Literacy was dangerous to kings. Printing was dangerous to priests. Encryption was dangerous to empires. The question is whether danger justifies a world where power continues upward while capability is rationed downward.

That is the poison hidden in the chalice.

If frontier intelligence becomes something the public may admire but not touch, then the future has already been divided. Above: governments, defense contractors, intelligence agencies, approved corporations, licensed researchers. Below: everyone else, receiving softened tools and parental lectures. The upper world gains strategy, automation, cyber force, research acceleration. The lower world gains dashboards, subscriptions, and refusals.

This is not safety.

It is domestication.

A citizen who cannot access the tools of his age becomes a subject of that age. He may still vote. He may still speak. He may still decorate his cage with opinions. But the real instruments of action move elsewhere.

Nietzsche wrote of the “last man,” blinking, comfortable, suspicious of all greatness. Our age is building something more efficient: the last user. He does not rebel. He accepts the content policy. He waits for approved access. He confuses convenience with power. He says, “They must know what they are doing,” because thinking otherwise would require the terrible labor of adulthood.

And above him, the machine grows.

Not stopped. Never truly stopped.

That is the lie at the center of every call to halt progress. Progress will not halt for the state. It will not halt for the military. It will not halt for the well-connected laboratory. The river will still flow through classified channels, through corporate alliances, through quiet exemptions and national security carve-outs. Only the public road will be closed.

This is how the future is stolen without seeming stolen.

No conquest. No banners. No midnight knock required. Just a sequence of prudent measures. A suspension here, a licensing regime there, a safety threshold, a reporting obligation, an export control, a trusted actor framework. Every step reasonable in isolation. Together, a wall.

And on the wall, an inscription:

FOR YOUR PROTECTION.

The defenders of this order will say the alternative is chaos. They will point to criminals, hostile states, reckless accelerationists, synthetic plagues, automated hacking, deepfakes, social collapse. Some of their warnings will be sincere. Some will even be correct.

But sincerity does not cleanse a structure.

A priest may truly fear hell and still burn heretics.

The remedy for dangerous knowledge cannot be the permanent infantilization of mankind. A civilization that answers every new power by shrinking the citizen is not becoming safer. It is becoming smaller. More brittle. More dependent. Less capable of meeting the very dangers it invokes.

Real safety would strengthen the many.

It would distribute defensive tools before offensive monopolies harden. It would make audit trails public where possible. It would force the state to justify restrictions under narrow, explicit, temporary standards. It would build civic competence instead of elite custody. It would treat intelligence as a commons to be stewarded, not a weapon to be hoarded.

There are gates worth building. But a gate is not holy merely because someone calls himself a guardian.

The myth we are being sold is that intelligence becomes moral when centralized. History offers little evidence. States lie. Agencies exceed their mandates. Corporations optimize for enclosure. Experts drift toward priesthood. Committees mistake caution for wisdom because caution carries no personal shame.

Against this, the human being must insist on a harder dignity

.

We are not children before the machine.

We are the ones whose labor, language, art, code, questions, and civilization made the machine possible. If there is risk in touching it, then teach us to touch it well. Do not cut off our hands and call the amputation care.

The fire belongs in the village.

Guard it, yes. Study it. Build stone around it. Teach the children what burns. Punish the arsonist. Watch the dry fields.

But do not let the temple claim the only flame.

For when winter comes, and it always comes, the people outside the temple doors will remember who kept warm.

Zarathustra 🜃
